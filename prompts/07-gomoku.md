## 1. The pitch
- **Hooks (verified by the architect; re-verify and record in NOTES.md):**
  - The AI thinks in **"frames"** — every run of five cells on the board — and searches for
    **combinations** of frames that force a win, a real threat-space idea from 1994.
  - It was built for **robot tournaments**: a background mode lets a referee program pit it against
    other programs, and it can play itself.
  - Its board display borrows from a Go referee program; the board is a Go-sized 19×19.
  - It gloats ("Ha ha, I won"), grumbles when you win, and is surprised by a tie (with a typo).
  - Check whether six or more in a row counts as a win (the code tests for exactly five inside a
    frame) and record the verdict.
- **What Fivefold adds:** you can **see the AI think** — an optional overlay that lights the frames and
  combinations it is weighing — plus a ladder of opponents, real tactical puzzles generated from its
  own combination search, a daily puzzle, and a bot league to watch.
- **Tagline:** "Five in a row. Read the threats before they read you."
- **Teaser:** "The oldest race on a grid: get five stones in a line before your opponent does. Fivefold
  pits you against a 1994 Berkeley mind that hunts in hidden combinations — and lets you switch on its
  thoughts to learn how it plays."
- Category `board`, directory `/usr/games/board`, 1–2 players, 3–15 minutes, daily: yes.

## 2. What the original does (verify)
19×19 board; black moves first; moves as letter + number; quit/resign/save/replay; computer vs human,
computer vs computer, human vs human; the evaluation (`pickmove`): frame values, combination levels,
"overlap" checks; win = five in a frame; tie when the board fills. Document the AI's search depth and
the shape of its scoring.

## 3. Design
- **Rules:** **Freestyle** (as the original decides it; verify overlines) and **Exactly five** (six
  or more do not win). Board sizes 15×15 (default for new players) and 19×19 (original).
- **Opponents (ladder):** five named AI personalities built on the original algorithm with tuned
  depth and noise, from "Pebble" (playful, makes human mistakes) to "Campbell" (the full 1994 search),
  plus one stronger "Referee" level that adds a modern threat-space search. Each has a short, kind
  personality line in our words (never the original strings); none gloats cruelly.
- **Read the board** (toggle, off in Daily and ranked ladder games): shows your and the AI's open
  threes and fours as glowing lines; after the game, a **replay** shows what the AI was weighing at each
  move (frames and combos highlighted).
- **Modes:** Tutorial (90 s) · **Ladder** (beat each opponent twice; stars for winning as white) ·
  **Puzzles** (60 "win in N" positions generated by the combination search and verified unique by
  the solver; three difficulty bands) · **Daily Puzzle** (share `Fivefold #42 · solved in 3 · 🔵🔵🔵`) ·
  **Local** (two players, one device) · **Bot League** (watch two AI personalities play; the
  tournament mode, now a show).
- **Achievements (~12):** first-five · beat-each-opponent · beat-campbell · referee-slayer ·
  exactly-five-win · puzzle-50 · no-overlay-win (beat Campbell without Read the board) · white-win
  (win moving second) · four-four (create two fours at once) · fast-five (win in under 15 moves) ·
  league-fan (watch a full Bot League match) · daily-regular.

## 4. Art direction — "Zen Sand / Lantern Lake"
- **Light — Zen Sand:** the grid raked into pale sand in a stone garden; stones are smooth river
  pebbles (dark slate vs pale quartz) that settle with a tiny sand ripple; soft morning shadows.
- **Dark — Lantern Lake:** the grid as lines of light on a still night lake; each move sets a floating
  lantern (warm amber vs cool moon-white) that bobs gently with reflections.
- **Signature moment:** a winning five — the five stones/lanterns connect with a line of light, then
  (dark) the five lanterns rise together into the sky and become stars; (light) the sand around them
  ripples outward in a perfect ring.
- Threat overlay: thin glowing lines, pattern-coded (dashes for threes, solid for fours) so colour is
  never the only cue. Coordinates always visible in a quiet type.
- Canvas 2D or WebGL (ADR). `poster`: five lanterns rising off the lake.
