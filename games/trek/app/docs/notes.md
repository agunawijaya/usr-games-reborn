Working notes: things the next contributor should know that are not a

## Port style name

`procedural-web` is not one of the recommended style slugs in root
[ADR-006](../../../../../docs/decisions/006-multi-port-architecture.md)
§Port Naming Conventions (`classic-web`, `fancy-web`, `mobile-gimmicks`,
…). ADR-006 has **no registration step** ("No central registry. First PR
merging a port under a given name locks that name"), so nothing had to be
filed and no root ADR was edited.

For a future revision of the ADR-006 style table, the proposed entry is:

| Slug | Fidelity | Platform | Aesthetic | Additions allowed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `procedural-web` | Additive OK | Web (browser) | Everything generated by code — zero image/audio assets (port ADR 002) | As `fancy-web` |

It is a `fancy-web` sibling that differs by asset policy, not by
platform. The name says so up front; `fancy-web-procedural` would also
fit the tech-suffix rule.

of the engine, because mechanical parity is the point of the comparison.

## Shared engine vs original combat rules

Checked against the C source (upstream `trek/phaser.c`, `attack.c`,
`torped.c`, `move.c`) during review round 2. These differences live in
the engine shared with fancy-web, so this port keeps them for parity.
Changing them means changing both ports (a decision for the owner, with
an ADR).

| Rule | Original BSD trek | Shared engine |
|---|---|---|
| Phaser volley | up to 6 banks fire at once, one per Klingon (automatic mode) | every Klingon hit at once ✓ (same shape) |
| Phaser energy split | weighted to the nearest, capped at the energy each kill needs, the rest reported as overkill | split equally, then distance attenuation |
| Phasers and shields | **can't fire with shields up** ("we cannot fire through shields") | allowed |
| Klingon fire | each Klingon with power ≥ 20 fires once per turn; hit ∝ its power, falls off with distance; firing drains its power | each fires once per turn ✓; damage drawn from a fixed range per ship class, reduced by distance; never tires |
| Klingon movement | move toward you before the attack, away after (`klmove`) | stationary |
| Arrival | Klingons in the new quadrant attack on arrival ✓, and the ones you leave get a parting shot | arrival attack only |
| Torpedoes | 1, or a burst of 3 with a spread up to 15° | 1 per command |

The volley shape (you fire everything, then every Klingon answers once)
is the same in both, and the visuals now follow it.

## Gotchas met while building this port

- **MSAA + `pow()` = black specks.** With a multisampled target the
  fragment shader runs at the pixel centre, which can fall just outside
  the triangle. The interpolated UV then goes slightly negative and
  `pow(negative, x)` is NaN. Thin edge-on quads (the engine plumes) made
  lines of black specks. Fix: clamp varyings before `pow`, and scrub NaN
  in the bloom prefilter and the composite (`clamp` / `max` return the
  other operand on D3D).
- **Bounding boxes lie.** `Box3.setFromObject` includes engine plumes
  and glow billboards (a 2 × 2 quad scaled only in the shader). Shield
  and explosion sizes come from `hullBox()`, which only counts lit hull
  meshes.
- **Destroyed ≠ gone.** The engine removes a destroyed Klingon at once.
  The renderer marks the victim `doomed` *synchronously in `play()`*,
  before `sync()` runs, or it vanishes before the torpedo arrives.
- **Playwright's fake clock keeps ticking.** `page.clock.install()` alone
  lets time run in real time; call `pauseAt()` straight after it, or
  "deterministic" screenshots drift by however long loading took.
- **A constant light count.** Three.js recompiles every material when the
  number of lights changes. Star and FX point lights come from fixed
  pools whose intensity goes to zero when idle. Lite hides them, which
  recompiles once, on the profile switch only.
- **Sky brightness varies a lot.** Raw bakes range over roughly 10× in
  mean luminance. Each bake is measured on a 32 × 20 probe and scaled
  towards a target (capped at 1.5×, so sparse skies stay dark).
- **Background stars vs navigation stars.** Bright embedded nebula stars
  are pushed out of the central 60 % of the sky and dimmed in play,
  because a big star inside the grid looks like an in-sector star when
  the HUD says "Stars here 0".

## Deferred / ideas

- A spiral-galaxy / galactic-core sky type, and composition rules that
  frame a hero object instead of only keeping it off the grid.
- Klingons don't move in this engine (inherited). If the engine ever
  gains movement, `Tactical.syncKlingons` needs a glide like the player's.
- A cross-fade between the old and new sky on reduced-motion warps
  (currently a fade to black).
- Lite could go further (Lambert hulls, no normal maps) if very weak
  machines need more than ~18 fps in combat.
- `computer` could draw the suggested course on the tactical grid, not
  just print it.
