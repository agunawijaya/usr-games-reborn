ATC — How to Play

Control Room 1986 · BSD atc(6) spiritual successor

The Job

You are an air traffic controller. Airplanes appear at exits or airports on your radar screen. Each plane has a destination (another exit, or an airport). Your job: route each plane to its destination — alive — by typing commands that adjust its altitude and heading. There is no win condition. You survive as long as you can, and your score is the number of planes you safely deliver.

The Radar Screen (Center)

The green radar shows a rectangular sector. Here's what each glyph means:

A5
An airplane in flight. Uppercase letter = jet (fast, ticks every update). Lowercase letter = prop (slow, ticks every other update). The small digit below (5) is the plane's altitude in thousands of feet (0–9 → 0–9000 ft). The arrow points in its current heading. Brightness = status: bright = MARKED (paying attention), dim = UNMARKED, faded = IGNORED.
*0
Beacon. A waypoint inside the arena. Useful as a routing landmark: you can command Attb0 to point a plane toward beacon 0. Beacons themselves do nothing to planes — they're navigation aids.
→A0
Airport. Where planes take off from and land at. The arrow shows the airport's runway direction — a plane landing here must be flying in exactly that direction, at altitude 0.
◦ 3
Exit. A numbered gate on the border of the arena. Planes departing here spawn at altitude 7000 ft. Planes exiting here must be at altitude 9000 ft, otherwise you lose.

Faint dashed lines are airways — visual guides connecting features. They have no gameplay effect, just help you plan routes. The dot grid shows every cell you can occupy.

The Command Line (Bottom)

Type commands character by character. The hint above the command line shows valid next tokens after every keystroke — you never have to memorise the grammar.

Every command starts with a plane letter (A–Z), then an action:

Aa5
Plane A, set altitude to 5000 ft.
Aa+3
Plane A, climb 3000 ft from current altitude.
Aa-2
Plane A, descend 2000 ft.
Atw
Plane A, turn to North (see compass below).
AtL
Plane A, hard left 90°.
AtR
Plane A, hard right 90°.
Attb0
Plane A, turn toward beacon 0.
Atta1
Plane A, turn toward airport 1.
Atte2
Plane A, turn toward exit 2.
Ac
Plane A, circle in place until you say otherwise.
Am / Ai / Au
Plane A, mark / ignore / unmark (brightness cues).

The Sidebar (Right)

Four cards, top to bottom:

1. Sector Selection. Buttons to switch between sectors. Easy is the training sector (slow, one airport, four exits). Default is Ed James's reference difficulty. Killer is the fast, punishing sector — do not start here.

2. Shift Status. SHIFT MM:SS counts down from 15 minutes (compressed 8-hour shift). NEXT TICK Ns shows how long until the game advances one tick — press Enter with an empty buffer to force it now.

3. Active Traffic. Every plane currently on your radar, with: origin → destination, altitude, heading, fuel. Fuel goes yellow at 12, red at 5 — those planes are urgent. This panel is your ground truth; don't rely on the radar alone.

4. Events. Rolling log of the last ~12 events (spawns, landings, exits, losses, commands you issued). Colour-coded.

How Time Works

Time advances in discrete ticks (every 5 seconds on Default, 6 on Easy, 3 on Killer). Between ticks the state is frozen — you have time to type commands. When a tick fires:

  • Every jet moves one cell in its heading direction.
  • Every prop moves one cell — but only on every other tick.
  • Altitude changes by ±1 toward the value you commanded.
  • Heading changes by up to ±2 (45°) toward the direction you commanded.
  • Fuel decreases by 1 for every plane that moved.

You cannot force a plane to change altitude by more than 1 per tick, or heading by more than 2 (90°). Plan ahead.

Winning a Plane

  • ✓ Airport landing: plane reaches an airport at altitude 0, flying in the airport's arrow direction.
  • ✓ Exit departure: plane reaches its destination exit at altitude 9 (9000 ft).

Losing the Shift

Any of these ends the game immediately:

  • ✗ Two planes within 1 cell of each other and within 1000 ft of altitude (collision).
  • ✗ Any plane runs out of fuel (fuel < 0).
  • ✗ Plane reaches its destination exit at wrong altitude (not 9).
  • ✗ Plane lands at wrong airport, or from wrong direction.
  • ✗ Plane leaves the arena through a border cell that isn't its destination exit.
  • ✗ Plane climbs above altitude 9 or descends to 0 outside an airport.

Walk-through: Your First Plane

1. A jet spawns: A at exit 2, altitude 7, heading West. Its destination is airport 0 (bottom of screen). Traffic panel confirms.
2. You need it to descend and turn south. Type A — hint shows valid actions.
3. Type a — hint asks for a digit. Type 0 — command is Aa0. Press Enter. Plane will now descend 1 per tick.
4. Type Atx — plane will turn toward South (x). Press Enter.
5. Wait for ticks. Plane moves south, descending. Right before it reaches airport 0, type Atta0 to align with the airport's runway direction, then Enter.
6. Plane arrives at altitude 0, matching runway direction → landed. +1 to score.

Tips for Beginners

  • Start on Easy. Do not touch Killer until you can land 10 planes on Easy.
  • Circle (Ac) is your friend. When a plane is early or you're overloaded, park it in a circle at high altitude.
  • The traffic panel is faster to read than the radar for altitude and fuel. Use it.
  • An empty Enter forces a tick. Use this to skip idle time when nothing is happening.
  • Fuel = arena width + arena height at spawn. On Easy this is 35 — you have time. On Killer, plan tighter routes.
