# Orchard Crawl — how to play

## Controls

| Action                           | Keys                       | Mouse                     |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Steer the worm                   | W A S D or arrow keys      | —                         |
| Pause and resume                 | Esc or P                   | the ledger’s Pause button |
| Crawl the next orchard at once   | Enter on the orchards page | ▶ Crawl                   |
| Crawl from a briefing            | Enter                      | Crawl                     |
| Crawl again (after a crawl)      | R                          | Crawl again               |
| Back to the Hall (after a crawl) | H                          | Back to the Hall          |
| Back to the orchards             | Backspace or Esc           | ← Orchards                |
| Start a challenge                | Enter on its briefing      | Start                     |
| Back to the Hall from the desk   | Esc on the orchards page   | the Hall’s strip          |

Between key presses the worm keeps crawling the way it faces, one cell at a time. It cannot turn
straight back on itself, and two quick presses are taken one after the other. A turn takes effect
from the cell the head is already crawling into, as in the port; when a press comes just as the
worm is about to hit something, the turn is taken at once instead.

## The rules

- **The orchard** is a grid of 30 by 20 cells. Its edge, any fence and every part of your own body
  are hard: crawl into one and the crawl is over.
- **Apples** carry a number from 1 to 9. Eat one and you grow by that many cells over your next
  moves (your tail stays put while you grow).
- **Points.** Each bite scores all the growing you still have to do, its own number included. Eat
  a 9 and you score 9; eat a 6 three moves later, while 6 of the 9 are still to come, and that bite
  scores 12. That is a chain; the ledger beside the field always says how much you are still
  growing, so you can see what a bite would be worth.
- **The harvest.** Each orchard of the season asks for a number of apples. Once you have eaten
  that many, your burrow opens on a free cell you can reach, glowing, with a small arrow by your
  head pointing the way.
- **Home.** Crawl into the burrow and the worm slides in after its head, out of reach of
  everything, and looks out of the dark once it is all in. The orchard is cleared; the next one
  opens.
- **After the harvest** you may keep eating for points first. Nothing makes you go in, but the
  orchard only gets busier and you only get longer.
- **Speed.** As in the port, the worm speeds up as it grows, from three moves a second at length 5
  to six at length 45 and beyond. The Steady pace setting keeps it at three.

## The creatures

| Creature        | What it does                                                                                                                           |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Frog            | Hops through now and then, sits a while, leaps a cell or two, and leaves. Catch it with your head for 50 points.                       |
| Over-ripe apple | An apple nobody eats for a while goes dark. It feeds and scores like any other.                                                        |
| Thief bird      | Swoops in for the biggest ripe apple in sight and carries it off. Eat that apple first and it leaves with nothing. It never hurts you. |
| Wasp            | Hatches from an apple left over-ripe for a few seconds and chases your head. Touch it and the crawl is over. One at a time.            |
| Rival worm      | Eats the same apples and plays by your rules. Its body is a wall to you and yours to it; two heads meeting end both crawls.            |
| Gardener        | Walks in from the far edge and follows the nearest worm, a step at a time, for a while. Do not let it reach your head. Fences stop it. |

## The season

Eight orchards, crawled in order. Each opens once the one before it is cleared.

| --- | --------- | -------------------------- | ------- | ------------------------------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |

| 2   | Savanna   | A full orchard, and a frog | 12      | open                            | 120      | Catch the frog                                               |
| 3   | River     | Fences                     | 12      | two long fences with gaps       | 140      | Come home shorter than 50                                    |
| 4   | Jungle    | The thief bird             | 13      | a cross of fence                | 140      | Send the bird away with nothing                              |

| 6   | Aztec     | A rival worm               | 13      | a fenced square with four gates | 150      | Make the rival worm crash                                    |
| 7   | Origami   | The gardener               | 14      | two H fences                    | 140      | Stay out of reach until the gardener goes                    |

**Stars.** Every orchard has three, earned only on a crawl that comes home: one for coming home,
one for scoring the orchard’s points, one for its feat. A star once earned is kept. The ledger
shows each star’s progress as you crawl, and says when a feat is done and all that is left is to
come home.

**The end of the season.** Clear Midnight and the season is over; its closing page stays on the
orchards page to be read again. Every orchard stays open for the stars still empty.

## The Daily Orchard

One crawl a day, the same for everyone: the orchard (the eight in turn), its apples’ numbers,
where they and the creatures appear and when, all drawn from the date. Your first finished crawl
of the day is the one that counts; it does not clear or open orchards of the season. The report
gives you a line to share, such as `Orchard Crawl #33 · Desert · home · 🍎13 · 214 pts · ★★☆`,
with no link in it.

## The free orchard

The port as it always played, with no harvest and no burrow: a crawl ends when you crash, as worm
always did. Pick one apple at a time (the 1980 rule) or a full orchard, the port’s three paces
(Classic, Fast, Speeds up), its six fences, which creatures come (with a full orchard; the frog
always does) and any of the eight looks. The best score is kept for each kind of orchard; past
100 points the Hall counts the crawl as a win.

## The challenges

Single crawls apart from the season, each with one goal; a crawl ends when the goal is met or a
rule is broken (or the worm crashes). Each tier opens the next in its family and is worth points
once; the whole orchard gives a medal for the most it was filled, crash or not. The three King
Drift families are weighted: a tier is worth its family’s weight plus its place, so the zigzag
gives 1, 2, 3 points, the lane 2, 3, 4 and no long straights 3, 4, 5. 81 points in all.

The challenges keep the pace they were made for (Steady pace does not apply), and the season,
the stars and the Daily Orchard are untouched by them.

## The almanac and records

The almanac has a page for the numbered apple, the over-ripe apple, the frog, the bird, the wasp,
the rival worm, the gardener and your burrow, each filled in the first time you really meet it
(eat one, catch it, see it take an apple, see one hatch, see the rival crash, outlast a gardener,
come home). Records keep each orchard’s stars and best crawl home, your Daily Orchards and the
totals.

## Settings

| Setting     | What it does                                                                                      |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sound       | The game’s own switch. In the Hall, the Hall’s volume and mute still decide how loud.             |
| Steady pace | The worm keeps three moves a second however long it grows, instead of speeding up as in the port. |

Reduced motion (the Hall’s setting, or the system’s) stills the drifting motes, the bobbing apples,
the twinkling stars and the sparks, and keeps only the rings that mark a bite or the burrow.

## Tips

- Count the cells to the next apple against the growing left in the ledger: an apple you can reach
  while still growing scores far more than the same apple a few moves later.
- Small numbers are the safe way to a harvest; big ones are the way to the points star. Mix them.
- The frog is worth five or six ordinary bites. It never stays long.
- In the Jungle, watch which apple the bird is heading for and eat it first.
- In the Desert, the dark apple is the one to eat next.
- A rival worm turns towards apples and away from walls: give it little room and it will run out
  of it before you do.
- Against the gardener, keep moving and put a fence between you.
